Research

A common thread runs through all of it: distributed training breaks in specific, diagnosable ways — communication cost, client drift, class imbalance, missing rare classes — and each of these papers isolates one of those failures, explains it theoretically, and fixes it with a method cheap enough to actually run.

P3

Fed-BRDECS: Privacy-Preserving and Heterogeneity-Aware Federated Deep Embedded ClusteringUnder review

Haemin Park, Diego Klabjan

In submission, AAAI

Deep embedded clustering needs global soft-assignment statistics — which, in a federated setting, means clients leaking their cluster composition. Replaces that objective with a locally computable sample-stability loss, adds prediction-balanced sampling to stop local majority clusters from dominating, and periodically restarts centroids to escape early collapse. Applied to federated time-series anomaly detection with no inference-time cost.

P2

Class-Grouped Normalized Momentum and Faster Hyperparameter Exploration to Tackle Class Imbalance in Federated Learning

Haemin Park, Diego Klabjan, Martin W. Braun, Xiuqi Li, Balakrishnan Ananthanarayanan

International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML), 2026

Privacy rules out the usual fixes for long-tailed data, so FedCGNM attacks imbalance at the optimizer instead: group classes by minimizing within-group variance, keep one momentum per group, normalize each to unit length. Every group gets equal pull without the scaling mismatch that breaks per-class normalization at scale. Ships with a convergence analysis under time-varying resampling rates and FedHOO, a bandit-based tuner that exploits federated parallelism to evaluate many rate combinations at linear cost. Evaluated on four long-tailed benchmarks and a proprietary chip-defect dataset.

P1

Communication-Efficient Federated Low-Rank Update Algorithm and its Connection to Implicit RegularizationUnder review

Haemin Park, Diego Klabjan

Under review, Transactions on Machine Learning Research (TMLR)

The first theoretical study of rank structure in federated learning: using random matrix theory, I show a client's Hessian carries a strictly higher stable rank than the server's, and that restricting local updates to a low-rank subspace improves cross-client gradient alignment. FedLoRU turns that into an algorithm — clients optimize low-rank factors, the server accumulates them into a higher-rank model — cutting communication from Kmn to Kr(m+n) while matching full-rank accuracy, and beating FedAvg outright once the client count passes 100.

The Advantage of Low-Precision and Low-Rank Training in Distributed Systems

Haemin Park, Diego Klabjan

Manuscript in preparation

Establishing that resource-efficient training — low precision, low rank — does not merely approximate full-resource training in large distributed settings but can outperform it, with a formal analysis of how that advantage scales.

Federated Continual Learning with Decoupled LoRA

Haemin Park, Diego Klabjan

Manuscript in preparation

Decoupling the magnitude and direction of low-rank updates and combining them with sharpness-aware minimization, so a model adapts quickly to new tasks while flatter, more stable update geometry limits catastrophic forgetting.
