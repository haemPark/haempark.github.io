About

I'm a PhD candidate in the Industrial Engineering & Management Sciences department at Northwestern University, advised by Diego Klabjan. My research sits where optimization theory, machine learning, and large-scale systems meet: I study how to train models efficiently and adaptively in federated and continual settings, where data is heterogeneous and imbalanced and training is bounded by real constraints such as communication and memory budgets. The goal is to make methods that are both theory grounded and actually deployable.

I also like tackling real-world problems with messy, challenging data under real constraints. I have worked with large-scale semiconductor manufacturing image data, and multi-dimensional, unaligned time-series data. I'm also always open to other interesting and challenging problems beyond these too.

PAPERS

ICML

Class-Grouped Normalized Momentum and Faster Hyperparameter Exploration to Tackle Class Imbalance in Federated Learning

Haemin Park, Diego Klabjan, Martin W. Braun, Xiuqi Li, Balakrishnan Ananthanarayanan

International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML), 2026

Privacy rules out the usual centralized fixes for long-tailed data, and clients with different local class distributions need different resampling choices. FedCGNM handles imbalance in the optimizer: it groups classes by minimizing within-group variance, keeps one momentum per group, and normalizes each group's contribution so minority classes aren't drowned out. FedHOO, a hierarchical bandit method, adapts client-specific resampling rates by exploiting federated parallelism. Includes a convergence analysis under time-varying resampling rates. Evaluated on long-tailed image, tabular, network-security, and semiconductor-defect data.

TMLR

Communication-Efficient Federated Low-Rank Update Algorithm and its Connection to Implicit Regularization

Haemin Park, Diego Klabjan

Transactions on Machine Learning Research (TMLR)

Federated clients optimize on small, heterogeneous local datasets, so full-rank local updates can follow client-specific directions that don't serve the global objective. Using effective dimensionality (Hessian stable rank), I analyze how local and global optimization geometry differ and when restricting local updates to a low-rank subspace acts as implicit regularization. FedLoRU turns this into an algorithm: clients train and communicate low-rank factors, and the server periodically accumulates them, so the model is never confined to one fixed subspace. It cuts communication, and its accuracy advantage over full-rank updates widens as the number of clients grows across the configurations evaluated, on image classification and language-model fine-tuning.

Privacy-Preserving and Heterogeneity-Aware Federated Deep Embedded ClusteringPreprint

Haemin Park, Diego Klabjan, Martin W. Braun, Xiuqi Li, Balakrishnan Ananthanarayanan

Federated deep clustering has to learn shared structure without pooling raw data, while client heterogeneity and aggregation noise destabilize assignments and centroids. Fed-BRDECS replaces global soft-assignment statistics with a locally computable sample-stability objective, uses prediction-balanced local sampling so rare predicted clusters stay represented, and restarts biased or inactive centroids. Applied to federated time-series anomaly detection with no additional inference-time cost.

WIP

Continual Learning with Decoupled Low-Rank Adaptation

Haemin Park, Diego Klabjan

How do the magnitude, direction, and displacement of low-rank updates relate to catastrophic forgetting? I study a parameterization that separates update size from a normalized low-rank direction. Decoupling the magnitude and direction of low-rank updates and combining them with sharpness-aware minimization, so a model adapts quickly to new tasks while flatter, more stable update geometry limits catastrophic forgetting.

WIP

Low-Bit Quantization of Few-Step Flow-Matching Models

Haemin Park, Diego Klabjan

Low-bit weight quantization perturbs a flow model's velocity predictions. With only a few large sampling steps, those errors redirect the trajectory and accumulate into the final sample, and reconstructing the weights accurately doesn't guarantee the velocity field is preserved. I'm developing a dual low-rank framework that separates shaping the weights before quantization from correcting the dynamics afterward, including timestep-specific corrections.

Project

Industrial Chip Anomaly Detection Intel

An end-to-end machine learning system for semiconductor defect and anomaly detection, built through my Intel internships and the Northwestern–Intel research project. Millions of sensor and image samples that are extremely imbalanced and structurally unlike standard vision datasets — defects are small, local, geometric, and rare, so natural image (e.g., ImageNet) intuitions mostly don't transfer.

Three pieces work together: federated learning, so distributed manufacturing sites train a shared defect model without images ever leaving the fab — under class imbalance severe enough to break standard aggregation; continual learning, so models already in production keep adapting as the process drifts, without retraining from scratch or forgetting what earlier lines taught them; and unsupervised time-series anomaly detection over multivariate sensor streams, where nothing is labeled and the failure modes aren't known in advance.

I contribute over 90% of the technical work — leading method development, implementation, experimentation, evaluation, and analysis, while collaborators supply domain requirements and feedback. Several papers in Publications come out of this system, two of them evaluated directly on its data.
