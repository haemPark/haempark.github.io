About

I'm a PhD candidate in the Industrial Engineering & Management Sciences department at Northwestern University, advised by Diego Klabjan. My research sits where optimization theory, machine learning, and large-scale systems meet: I study how to train models efficiently and adaptively in federated and continual settings, where data is heterogeneous and imbalanced and training is bounded by real constraints such as communication and memory budgets. The goal is to make methods that are both theory grounded and actually deployable.

I also like tackling real-world problems with messy, challenging data under real constraints. I have worked with large-scale semiconductor manufacturing image data, and multi-dimensional, unaligned time-series data. I'm also always open to other interesting and challenging problems beyond these too.

Research interests

Federated & Distributed Training Learning across clients that never share raw data — and staying stable as that population scales

Papers

P3

Privacy-Preserving and Heterogeneity-Aware Federated Deep ClusteringUnder review

Haemin Park, Diego Klabjan, Martin W. Braun, Xiuqi Li, Balakrishnan Ananthanarayanan

Under Review

Deep embedded clustering needs global soft-assignment statistics — which, in a federated setting, means clients leaking their cluster composition. Replaces that objective with a locally computable sample-stability loss, adds prediction-balanced sampling to stop local majority clusters from dominating, and periodically restarting mechanism to escape early collapse. Applied to federated time-series anomaly detection with no additional inference-time cost.

P2

Class-Grouped Normalized Momentum and Faster Hyperparameter Exploration to Tackle Class Imbalance in Federated Learning

Haemin Park, Diego Klabjan, Martin W. Braun, Xiuqi Li, Balakrishnan Ananthanarayanan

International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML), 2026

Privacy rules out the usual fixes for long-tailed data, so FedCGNM attacks imbalance at the optimizer instead: group classes by minimizing within-group variance, keep one momentum per group, normalize each to unit length. Every group gets equal pull without the scaling mismatch. Come up with a convergence analysis under time-varying resampling rates and FedHOO, a bandit-based tuner that exploits federated parallelism to evaluate many rate combinations at linear cost.

Paper
P1

Communication-Efficient Federated Low-Rank Update Algorithm and its Connection to Implicit RegularizationUnder review

Haemin Park, Diego Klabjan

Under review

The first theoretical study of rank structure in federated learning: using random matrix theory, I show a client's Hessian carries a strictly higher stable rank than the server's, and that restricting local updates to a low-rank subspace improves cross-client gradient alignment. FedLoRU turns that into an algorithm — clients optimize low-rank factors, the server accumulates them into a higher-rank model — cutting communication cost while matching full-rank accuracy. Interesting finding that theory and empirical results coincide: lower-rank updates perform better than full-rank updates when the number of clients is large.

Federated Continual Learning with Decoupled LoRA

Haemin Park, Diego Klabjan

Decoupling the magnitude and direction of low-rank updates and combining them with sharpness-aware minimization, so a model adapts quickly to new tasks while flatter, more stable update geometry limits catastrophic forgetting.

Project

Industrial Chip Anomaly Detection Intel

An end-to-end machine learning system for semiconductor defect and anomaly detection, built through my Intel internships and the Northwestern–Intel research project. Tens of thousands of lines of Python over millions of sensor and image samples that are extremely imbalanced and structurally unlike standard vision datasets — defects are small, local, geometric, and rare, so ImageNet intuitions mostly don't transfer.

Three pieces work together: federated learning, so distributed manufacturing sites train a shared defect model without images ever leaving the fab — under class imbalance severe enough to break standard aggregation; continual learning, so models already in production keep adapting as the process drifts, without retraining from scratch or forgetting what earlier lines taught them; and unsupervised time-series anomaly detection over multivariate sensor streams, where nothing is labeled and the failure modes aren't known in advance.

Working with one or two researchers and managers, I contribute over 90% of the technical work — leading method development, implementation, experimentation, evaluation, and analysis, while collaborators supply domain requirements and feedback. Several papers in Publications come out of this system, two of them evaluated directly on its data.
