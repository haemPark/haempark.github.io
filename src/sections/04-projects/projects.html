Industrial Chip Anomaly Detection Intel

An end-to-end machine learning system for semiconductor defect and anomaly detection, built through my Intel internships and the Northwestern–Intel research project. Tens of thousands of lines of Python over millions of sensor and image samples that are extremely imbalanced and structurally unlike standard vision datasets — defects are small, local, geometric, and rare, so ImageNet intuitions mostly don't transfer.

Three pieces work together: federated learning, so distributed manufacturing sites train a shared defect model without images ever leaving the fab — under class imbalance severe enough to break standard aggregation; continual learning, so models already in production keep adapting as the process drifts, without retraining from scratch or forgetting what earlier lines taught them; and unsupervised time-series anomaly detection over multivariate sensor streams, where nothing is labeled and the failure modes aren't known in advance.

Working with one or two researchers and managers, I contribute over 90% of the technical work — leading method development, implementation, experimentation, evaluation, and analysis, while collaborators supply domain requirements and feedback. Several papers in Publications come out of this system, two of them evaluated directly on its data.
