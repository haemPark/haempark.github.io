Industrial ML Automation System Intel

An end-to-end machine learning system for semiconductor manufacturing, built through my Intel internships and the Northwestern–Intel research project. Tens of thousands of lines of Python over millions of sensor and image samples that are extremely imbalanced and structurally unlike standard vision datasets — defects are small, local, geometric, and rare, so ImageNet intuitions mostly don't transfer.

Three pieces work together: continual learning so deployed models keep adapting as production shifts, federated learning so distributed manufacturing data trains a shared model without leaving its site, and diffusion models that generate realistic rare anomalies to fill in what the dataset never captured.

Working with one or two researchers and managers, I contribute over 90% of the technical work — leading method development, implementation, experimentation, evaluation, and analysis, while collaborators supply domain requirements and feedback. Two of my papers are evaluated on data from this system.

FedLoRU

Federated training where clients optimize low-rank factors and the server accumulates them into a higher-rank model. Communicates as little as 21% of the full-rank parameter count at matched accuracy, and overtakes full-rank FedAvg outright once the federation passes 100 clients. Composes cleanly with quantization.

FedHOO

Hyperparameter tuning built for federations too small to brute-force. An X-armed-bandit search exploits the parallelism already present across clients, evaluating many candidate configurations per round at linear rather than combinatorial cost.
