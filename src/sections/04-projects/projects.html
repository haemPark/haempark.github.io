Industrial AI for Semiconductor Automation Intel

An industrial machine learning project with Intel to automate defect and anomaly detection in semiconductor manufacturing, built through my Intel internships and the Northwestern–Intel research collaboration. It works on real manufacturing data — millions of chip images and sensor samples — not a curated benchmark.

That data doesn't look like natural-image datasets. Defects are small, local, and geometric, classes are extremely imbalanced, and intuitions from natural images (e.g., ImageNet) mostly don't transfer. So rather than assume a standard recipe, much of the work is finding the architecture and optimization methodology that fit this data: evaluating several architectures for inference latency, energy efficiency, and robustness to class imbalance; building imbalance-aware training from resampling, scheduling, and optimizer design; and adapting diffusion models to chip geometry to synthesize the defects that are too rare to collect.

The manufacturing setting also calls for the two learning paradigms that run through my research. Federated learning lets distributed manufacturing sites train a shared model without data ever leaving the fab, under class imbalance severe enough to break standard aggregation, and extends to unsupervised anomaly detection on sensor streams. Continual learning keeps models already in production adapting as the process drifts, without retraining from scratch or forgetting what earlier lines taught them.
